What you get

The right access for everyone

Managers, teachers, the office, learners and parents, each seeing exactly what they should. A teacher sees and marks the learners in their own classes; a head of year looks after their years; the office never sees anyone’s work or marks.

A page for every class

A manager makes each class with its teachers and learners. Each class has a stream for news and questions, its classwork by topic, a gradebook and rubrics. Post to one class, several or all of them at once.

One login, a wall between

A learner who also has a private tutor keeps one login. Your school sees only what it set, and so does the tutor.

Real mocks, on any device

Locked-down mocks in the desktop app or watched in a browser, an exam room to run them for a whole class, extra time for learners who need it, and grade boundaries per exam and session.

Reports for every family

Weekly report cards, approved by the teacher, in each parent’s free account or by WhatsApp and email.

Prof for every teacher

StudyBridge’s teaching assistant makes work, drafts marking and plans with each teacher, and nothing reaches a learner until the teacher approves it.

A timetable for everyone

Set out your school day, terms and each class’s lessons once (Week A and Week B too), or paste them from a spreadsheet. Cover for teachers who are away, and the class is told about room changes. Everyone sees their own week.

Registers and behaviour

A register every lesson, and live lessons fill theirs in as learners join. Parents hear the same day about an absence. Positives and concerns reach learners and parents; staff notes stay with staff.

The school office

A daily register in each homeroom, morning and afternoon if you take two. Parents say in their app when their child will be away, so it shows on every register and nobody worries; the office accepts it or not. Each learner’s contacts, health, learning support and photo permission, kept up to date by their family, with what teachers need to know on hand.

A gradebook that adds it up

Weight each class’s categories (tests 60%, homework 40%), mark work formative or summative, and see each learner’s overall with a suggested grade. Teachers set predicted grades and share them with learners and parents when they choose.

An assessment calendar

Every class’s tests and exams in one place. A learner with two on one day, or three in a week, shows straight away, and teachers are warned before they set another.

Term reports

Each class’s teachers write a grade, effort and a comment, with hints from their marks, attendance and points (IB MYP classes use criteria A to D). Your logo, colour and the head’s signature on every one, proofread before it goes out, and a transcript of every term. Parents are told, and it prints as a PDF.

Planners, and IB criteria

Units of work for each class, with the MYP’s or the PYP’s parts, a plan for every lesson, and the PYP programme of inquiry. Work can be marked on MYP criteria, 0 to 8 each, as well as marks.

IB core and the PYP

CAS, the Extended Essay and TOK; MYP Personal and Community Projects and Service as Action; the PYP Exhibition. Learners keep a journal, supervisors approve and give feedback, and coordinators see who’s behind. PYP units of inquiry, the programme of inquiry and Learner Profile notes too.

Classroom, complete

Files from Google Drive or OneDrive with a copy for each learner; voice, video and screen recordings in answers, feedback and class posts; fill in the blanks, matching, ordering and labelling questions; peer review that teachers check first; and similar answers pointed out while marking (compared with work handed in at your school, never the internet).

Families in touch

Parents write to their child’s teachers or the office in their free app (you choose who they write to first), and book parents’ evenings online, in person or by video, around each teacher’s times. Each learner keeps a portfolio of their best work. Print a parent code letter for every family in a class.

The whole school at a glance

Managers see attendance by year and class, who’s below 90%, which registers aren’t taken yet today, and behaviour across the school.
